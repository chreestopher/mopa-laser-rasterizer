Useful without physical color

The source image can contain many colors even when the final engraving will be monochrome. Rasterizer uses those colors to recognize and separate areas of the design. Each region can then receive a different hatch angle, line density, layer setting, or geometric treatment so neighboring areas remain visually distinct through tone, texture, grain, reflectivity, or viewing direction.

Hatch Palettes for monochrome contrast

Hatch Palettes are designed for work where source-image colors select different line patterns instead of different physical engraved colors. Every swatch can share one tested base laser setting while its hatch angle and optional line interval vary.

This is particularly useful for illustrations, maps, logos, diagrams, shaded artwork, and decorative engravings where direction and density should distinguish adjacent regions. The preview shows line color as the artwork-matching identifier, line direction as hatch angle, and relative spacing as line density.

Layered SVG without LightBurn

If your machine does not use LightBurn—or you prefer not to upload proprietary laser settings—use SVG-only mode. Rasterizer still performs image reduction, region construction, and preset processing, then provides layered vector artwork without assigning production parameters.

Import the SVG into software appropriate for your machine. Confirm its interpretation of layer colors, fills, scale, closed paths, placement, and every production setting before engraving.

LightBurn projects with tested settings

For a compatible LightBurn-controlled laser, select or upload a Material Library containing settings tested on the exact machine, lens, material, focus, and process. Rasterizer matches recognized swatch names to library descriptions and copies those settings into the generated .lbrn2 layers.

The name of this project does not imply that every workflow requires a MOPA source. Q-switched fiber, diode, CO2, UV, and other systems may use the vector-preparation features their controllers and materials support. Laser engraving tool questions

Do I need a MOPA laser?

No. MOPA sources are especially useful for controlled metal-color experiments, but Rasterizer's vector, hatch, texture, and SVG workflows can support other laser types when the machine, controller, software, material, and settings are compatible.

Do source colors become engraved colors?

Not necessarily. In a monochrome Hatch Palette workflow, source colors are classification labels that decide which pattern a region receives. The physical marks may share one nominal color while differing in texture or tone.

Does Rasterizer operate my laser?

No. It prepares artwork and optional LightBurn project settings. The operator must inspect, frame, test, and run the job in appropriate laser-control software.

Can I use it without LightBurn?

Yes. SVG-only mode exports layered vector artwork and leaves all machine-setting assignment to your production software.

Laser safety: Generated artwork and imported settings require operator review and material testing. Follow the laser and material manufacturers' limits, use appropriate guarding and extraction, supervise operation, and maintain fire precautions.
