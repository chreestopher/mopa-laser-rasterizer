Directional engraving preparation

Parallax

Convert the current depthmap into Krasnow-style horizontal angle ramps, inspect exactly what will remain unengraved, and export raster or grating geometry.

Shorter rampsLonger ramps
Exact far onlyMore background

The parallax export applies Ben Krasnow's horizontal depth-edge conversion in this browser. The cutoff marks the farthest portion of an AI-generated map as unengraved background; strength controls the length of the angle ramps at its boundaries. Existing depthmaps use their explicit pure-white or transparent background instead.

The Parallax PNG is an 8-bit angle map. The Parallax SVG converts that map into black, square-cell grating geometry but does not embed laser settings.

Experimental specular engraving preparation

Specular Scratch Hologram

Sample the current depthmap into open circular scratches. Under a small directional light, their moving reflections are intended to make nearer and farther parts of the image appear at different virtual depths.

This is a specular scratch experiment, not a diffraction grating or a relief-depth toolpath. Inspired by Aaron Se's HoloZens experiments, Rasterizer independently converts its edited depthmap into open Cut/Line arcs and places the virtual image behind the material surface. Rasterizer automatically increases overly dense sampling to keep the export below 30,000 arcs. Test a small sample on polished material with directional lighting before committing valuable stock.

Important limitation: a single photograph cannot provide measured physical depth. The model infers relative depth from visual cues and can misread reflections, transparency, shadows, abstract artwork, or unfamiliar objects. Always inspect and edit the result before using it for relief engraving. The uploaded image is processed locally; the browser downloads the AI model from Hugging Face when first used.
